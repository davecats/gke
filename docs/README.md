# Documentation of the branch dev_xinju

* [gke_particles.tex](gke_particles.tex) — the plan for a consistent treatment of the particles in the GKE: flow solver and stored data, what is computed today and why the budget is not yet closed, the whole-domain and fluid-phase formulations, numerical consistency and validation, implementation steps and open questions (build with `latexmk -pdf gke_particles.tex`).

## Current status (October 2026)

Implemented and validated:
* reading of the xinju dataset (raw MPI-rank files, see *dataset.cpl*); runtime parameters in *gke.in*;
* single-point statistics (step 1) and GKE (steps 2 and 3) averaged over the fluid phase: points and pairs of points inside a particle are excluded, every average is renormalized by the number of accumulated points or pairs;
* mean velocity (U,V,W)(y) of the fluid phase, including a non-zero mean wall-normal velocity, in the fluctuations, the transport by the mean flow and the production terms;
* standard second-order finite differences consistent with the staggered solver (zero discrete divergence);
* two implementations of step 2 that agree to round-off: FFT cross-correlations of mask-weighted fields (about 1 minute per snapshot of the xinju dataset on 24 cores) and the direct pair accumulation (reference; OpenMP and OpenACC);
* validation against independent numpy implementations and a regression test on a synthetic case (*test/*).

Missing (see *gke_particles.tex*):
* the immersed-boundary force of the solver (a source term 2⟨δu_i δf_i⟩ in the GKE and ⟨u_i f_j⟩+⟨u_j f_i⟩ in the single-point budgets);
* the fluxes through the particle surfaces generated by restricting the averages to the fluid phase, the effect of the renormalization by the pair fraction, and the conditional-mean terms ⟨δu_i⟩, ⟨δp⟩ over fluid pairs;
* post-processing that checks the closure of the budgets;
* a fully periodic variant (no walls) for settling particles in periodic boxes.

## Answers of the data provider (X. Jiang)

* The IB force `fib` has the units of an acceleration: the solver integrates the non-conservative form of the incompressible Navier–Stokes equations coupled with the classical (direct-forcing) IBM.
* The spreading kernel is three grid cells wide (Roma, Peskin & Berger 1999), centred symmetrically on the Lagrangian markers at the particle interface; see Appendix A of Jiang, Xu & Zhao, *J. Fluid Mech.* **1000**, A49 (2024), which describes the numerical method.

## Facts established on the data

* The variables of each rank file are stored as consecutive blocks; the conversion script `convert_h5.py` distributed with the data reshapes them with the variable as fastest index and produces scrambled HDF5 files (correct: `raw.reshape((n1m, n2drm, n3dcm, nvar), order="F")[..., k]`).
* The staggered velocity is divergence-free to round-off everywhere, inside the particles too.
* The level set is negative in the solid and positive in the fluid but is not a distance (|∇φ| ≈ 10 near the surface; it saturates at 0.3).
* The IB force is concentrated in a layer about two cells wide on the fluid side and three cells wide on the solid side of the surface.
